Built 2026-10-23. The stage entry is `dataset/build/s11_energy.py`; the method is the package `dataset/build/s11_energy/` (`method.py` volumes, balance, RAS and the 90 percent rule; `domestic.py` domestic basic prices; `sources.py` readers; `checks.py` hard checks; `config.py` strict config). The method is GTAP 12 ch.12 (text: `docs/gtap_method_sources/GTAP12_ch12_energy.txt`). David chose GTAP on energy trade and output values (2026-10-23 18:34 UTC+7) and source-data domestic prices (2026-10-23 19:12 UTC+7), and GTAP's order for gas at net importers (2026-10-23 20:13 UTC+7).

## Steps, in the GTAP order

1. **Volumes** (§12.2). PREP_energy_volumes 2022 (EIA INTL, JODI, UNSD fills) on the S01 regions: coal Mt, crude and products million bbl, gas bcm at the EI 40 MJ/m3 GCV basis, electricity TWh. NGPL goes to pet (E29). A blank trade volume is zero and flagged (`BLANK-TRADE-VOLUME`; the S10 value and the volume it would imply are reported in `volume_fills_s11.csv`, §12.4.2: totals come from the balance); a blank production is zero and flagged.
2. **Supply and use** (§12.2.4). Fix round w16 (choice `energy_supply_use_balance`): in every region whose domestic use is measured, supply and use are rescaled in opposite senses so they balance: P and M × a, D and X / a, a = sqrt((D + X) / (P + M)). Measured use D: crude, the refinery intake (JODI REFINOBS, else PREP crude use, else UNSD 4100/086) plus the UNSD direct users of `crude_direct_users.csv`, where the intake is measured; coal and gas, the EIA total consumption. pet and ely are not balanced (JODI product demand counts NGL and biofuels outside PREP refinery output; EIA electricity net consumption leaves out losses and own use), and their gaps are reported (`S11-Rsupuseexcl`). A region with no measured use, or with measured use and exports both zero, keeps the earlier one-sided rule: where exports exceed production plus imports, P and M × g and X / g, g = sqrt(X / (P + M)); exports with no supply are zero (`SUPPLY-USE-RESCALE`, `supply_use_s11.csv`). Refinery intake and direct use carry the region's factor, so they add up to the crude absorption. Alternatives: crude only, only where supply exceeds measured use, or `off` (the one-sided rule everywhere).
3. **Re-exports** (§12.2.5): rx = X · M / (P + M), removed from both exports and imports (`reexports_s11.csv`). Fable QA D-04 (David 2026-10-25, "do all fixes"; choice `reexport_removal`): where the region's customs report re-exports (UN Comtrade 2022, `data/un_comtrade_rx_2022`; every member of a composite), rx = X × RX / X of the good's lines (net weight, else value) instead of the same propensity (USA crude 3.1 percent, not 34; ARE products 21.1 percent); rx is at most min(X, M) and the same volume leaves the hub's X and M. `reexports_s11.csv` adds `basis`, `observed_share` and `reexports_same_propensity`.
4. **World balance** (§12.2.4): world exports = world imports by one factor per good (exports × f, imports / f), solved jointly with the regional factors of step 2 and the bound domestic exports at most production (production is raised where the bound binds), to a fixed point. `balance_s11.csv` has the world totals. `supply_use_balance_s11.csv` has each region and balanced good before and after: volumes, and export, domestic-sales and output values at the S11 prices (`S11-Rsupuse`, flags `SUPPLY-USE-TWO-SIDED`). `S11-Hsupuse` (hard) checks P + M = D + X in every measured region (1e-9), and refinery intake plus direct use against the oil absorption of `energy_values_s11.csv` (1e-6). Where the balance leaves a good with no domestic sales, its exporter's routes in `vfob_energy_s11.csv.gz` are rounded to add up to the output value, so the S13 target equals the exports S13 sums.
5. **Export prices** (§12.3.6): crude by EI 2022 marker per exporter; products by EI regional marker plus regional refining margin; gas and coal by BACI 2022 unit values (gas at 1.36 bcm per Mt LNG), except gas exporters whose BACI tonnes are a Comtrade estimate (choice `gas_price_estimated_tonnes`, `config/s11/gas_export_price_observed.csv`; TKM fix round, David 2026-10-24 00:40 UTC+7): their price is the BACI value over an observed volume of the same flows, either the exporter's S11 volume-source exports where BACI covers all its flows, or the EI 2025 inter-regional 2022 route volume (TKM: Other CIS pipeline exports to China, 39.756 bcm, over the BACI value of TKM, KAZ and UZB to China). TKM, KAZ, UZB, CAN and USA are listed (KAZ, UZB, CAN and USA by David's card in the fix thread, 2026-10-24 05:14 UTC+7; CAN and USA on their volume-source exports); electricity by BACI HS 271600 value over EIA export TWh. A missing export price is the simple average of exporters (ch.12 p.19).
6. **Bilateral volumes** (§12.4.2): the S10 VFOB over export prices is the seed, RAS to the balanced vectors (1e-6). Fable QA D-02 (choices `export_price_by_route`, `gas_route_volume_observed`, `gas_import_origin`): each route is priced at its own BACI 2022 unit value where it has one within 1/3 to 3 times the exporter's reference price of step 5 (else the reference; gas exporters with estimated tonnes and electricity at the reference on every route), both for the seed (S10 VFOB over the route price) and for the value (RAS volume × route price), so a route the RAS keeps keeps its S10 value; the exporter's export price in `energy_values_s11.csv` is its VFOB over its volume (the domestic-price rules keep the reference as the trade-parity price). RUS and MMR pipeline gas to China take the EI 2022 route volume for the line China reports without tonnes (`config/s11/gas_route_volume_observed.csv`). The DEU gas seed column is Eurostat nrg_ti_gas 2022 by partner, its no-origin volume shared by the partners' nrg_te_gas reports (`config/s11/gas_import_origin.csv`, `data/eurostat_gas_trade`); TUR takes its own Eurostat partner report (RU 21.6 bcm, absent from BACI). `S11-Rroute`, `S11-Rgasorigin` report them. Empty rows or columns get a seed of 1e-6 of the exporter's volume spread by import shares (`RAS-SEED`).
7. **CIF, margins and duty**: CIF = FOB × (1 + S08 pair margin rate); VST rescaled by mode; VMSB = CIF × (1 + S10 tm); a pair S10 never priced takes zero inside a customs union, else the importer's MFN-based rate (`S11-Rnewpair`).
8. **Domestic basic prices** (§12.3.4–12.3.8, `domestic.py`), per commodity and region, in order:
   1. source data (`config/s11/domestic_price_sources.csv`): EI 2025 Henry Hub (USA gas) and NBP (GBR gas); AER ST98 AECO-C (CAN gas, CAD at WDI PA.NUS.FCRF CAN); Eurostat nrg_pc_203 band I3 gas and nrg_pc_205 band ID electricity, excluding taxes and levies, mean of 2022-S1 and S2, single-country regions; EIA Annual Coal Report 2022 table 28 (US coal, mine price); NBS yearbook 2023 coal mining revenue over raw coal output (China coal, CNY at WDI); EIA Electric Power Annual 2022 table 2.4 (US industrial electricity); EIA 2022 spot gasoline, diesel, jet and propane weighted by 2022 US refinery and blender net production (US petroleum, §12.3.9; gas-plant propane is the NGPL add-on); transcribed cited prices (`config/s11/transcribed_prices_2022.csv`, source kind `transcribed`, fix round A9): RUS crude Urals 76.09 USD/bbl, RUS gas the midpoint of the 37-48 bn regulated value over 484 bcm domestic use (88 USD per thousand m3), SAU methane 1.25 USD/MMBtu, IND coal 1,642 INR/t at WDI PA.NUS.FCRF IND (20.9 USD/t). Each item names its commodity, region, unit and (for LCU) exchange-rate country; a unit, item, rate or region that does not match stops the run (`S11-Htranscribed`). At a net importer with imports priced, the import basic price comes ahead of a purchasers' price (§12.3.8; David 20:13); hub and mine prices keep their place and electricity stays on Eurostat. Purchasers' prices become basic prices by eq.(2) with the tax zero and the Table 12.8 industrial margin (gas 1.30, electricity 0). EUR at WDI PA.NUS.FCRF DEU 2022.
   2. IEA price gap (David's choice), booked as GTAP books fossil-fuel consumption subsidies (fix round B15, David's card 2026-10-24 14:13 UTC, choice `fuel_subsidies`; Chepeliev, McDougall and van der Mensbrugghe 2018, JGEA 3(1) §3, used by GTAP 12 ch.3 §3.2.4): the domestic basic price is the reference (trade-parity) price, and the IEA 2022 subsidy per unit of consumption (real 2024 USD × WDI US GDP deflator 2022/2024) is a consumption subsidy that S13 books as a negative purchase tax, so the tax-paid price users pay (reference less gap) is unchanged and output values rise to the reference price. The reference is the export price for a net exporter, the import basic price for a net importer, and the exporters' simple average where there is no trade. A gap not below the reference is not used (`S11-Rgapunused`). `fuel_subsidies_s11.csv` gives, per region and commodity, the reference and consumer prices, the gap per unit and the subsidy = (reference − consumer price) × domestic use, the domestic use being the consumption volume the gap is measured on, or the S11 absorption (production + imports − exports) where that is smaller (the paper's step 1: the data base volume replaces the outside volume where the outside one is larger). `S11-Hsubsidy` (hard) checks the identity, that each IEA-gap price is the reference before the 90 percent rule (a lignite blend excepted), and that every IEA-gap row has a subsidy row. `subsidy_s11.csv` marks the IEA countries whose subsidy is booked. With `fuel_subsidies` set to `off` the gap lowers the basic price and nothing is booked (the rule before B15).
   **Extension (David 2026-10-24, "stick to GTAP"; follows GTAP, the paper's §3.2 steps 1-2):** every other region and good with an IEA 2022 subsidy is booked too: rows priced from cited source data (the A9 regulated prices, RUS and SAU gas; Eurostat and other source rows in IEA countries) and rows whose gap was not used or could not be formed (gap not below the reference, as IRN gas and ely and RUS ely; no PREP consumption volume, as IRN and RUS pet). After rules 3-6 the row's S11 price stays the consumer price. The rate is the region's IEA value (members summed, at 2022 prices) per unit of the PREP consumption volume, or of the S11 absorption where PREP has no consumption volume; domestic use is the smaller of consumption and absorption (step 1). Step 2 (Table 2 rows 10-11): where the rate is above the reference price and imports exceed half of domestic use, the rate is the reference price. The market (domestic basic) price is consumer price + rate (the paper's PM1 = PM0 + VS/ED), marked `+iea_subsidy` in the rule, and the subsidy is rate × domestic use, never above the IEA value. The price-gap value (reference − consumer price) × domestic use is written beside it (`price_gap_subsidy_musd`); where the two disagree the IEA value is booked, as the paper books the IMF value, so the market price of such a row is the reference only where they agree. The exporter cap is not applied again on top of the subsidy (a net exporter's market price can exceed its export price, as in the paper). The value `iea_price_gap_regions_at_reference_price_gap_booked_as_consumption_subsidy_s13_negative_purchase_tax` restores the gap-rows-only rule. `S11-Rsubsidyext` lists the extension rows (IEA, booked and price-gap values, step 2 rescalings); `S11-Rsubsidyunbooked` lists IEA subsidies with no row.

## Running

`cd dataset/build && python3 s11_energy.py` (about two minutes; packages in `requirements-s11.txt`). Float outputs are rounded to 1e-6 so hashes match across machines. `GTAP_STAGES_DIR=<dir>` runs it against a scratch copy of the upstream stages.
