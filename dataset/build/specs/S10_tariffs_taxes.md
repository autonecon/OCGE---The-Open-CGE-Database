# S10 protection, support and taxes

Rebuilt 2026-10-23. The stage entry is `dataset/build/s10_tariffs_taxes.py`; the method is the package `dataset/build/s10_tariffs/`; the screens are `dataset/build/screens/s10_suite.py`; the mutation tests are `dataset/build/tests/test_s10_checks.py`. ## Tariffs (GTAP 12 ch.11B), in the GTAP order

1. **Schedules.** TRAINS MFN schedules from the manifest (`sources.load_mfn`): Reported where `NBR_NA_LINES` is 0, else AVEEstimated; a specific duty with no AVE has no rate and is left out of the weight. EU members share the EUN schedule. Tunisia is the 2016 Reported schedule raised by the 2018 finance law (art. 39); on Turkish industrial goods the Türkiye–Tunisia FTA Protocol I applies: zero, except List III (never reduced, MFN) and the List II goods in the chapters of Finance Law 2018 art. 40 (90% of MFN from 2018, phased out over three years from 2022 by Finance Law 2020 art. 52: 60% of MFN in 2022) (`tunisia_schedule`). New Caledonia is its own tariff consolidated on 1 January 2023, DD only, with the 2022 DD restored on the three HS6 that DRDNC notice 22001425 shows changed on 1 January 2023, and EU and OCT origin free of DD (`ncl_schedule`). The 2022 HTS Special K and L columns put an erga omnes zero on the US MFN, on every rate-bearing national line (`usa_pharma_appendix`).
2. **Named overrides on the schedule, before the minimum with preferences** (`rates.apply_overrides`): Zambia class A lines (ad valorem alternative), Egypt 240220 (EGP 100/kg over the BACI 2022 FOB unit value), Korea soy, crude, LNG, article 71 and the TRQ fill rule, Morocco wheat suspension, Cambodia 271012, Iraq Resolution 393 bands with the food zero, Panama-US TPA and the PRK flat rate, and Egypt 330210 and 210690 at the non-alcoholic national line of Decree 218/2022 (5 and 10 percent; TRAINS averages in the 3000 percent alcoholic compound preparations, `egypt_mixed_sublines`, fix list B3, our choice). Every override is a hard check (`S10-Hoverride`). The Korea TRQ fill keeps the MAFRA plan measure (`trq_fill_measure`, David 2026-10-22); the alternative `baci_2022_hs6_tonnes_over_quota` (Guimbard et al. 2012 p.10 measure: BACI 2022 tonnes over the quota, `overrides.KOREA_TRQ_BACI_T`) is implemented and puts rice at the out-of-quota rate.
   **TRQ fill rule outside KOR and PAN** (GTAP 12 ch.11B `trq_fill_rule`; `trq_outside_kor_pan`, `trq_line_rates`, `trq_no_fill`, `trq_bilateral_fill`; `s10_tariffs/trq.py`, round-3 fix wE2). TRAINS HS6 simple averages for CAN, CHE, JPN and CHN mix the in-quota and over-quota national lines. The quota lines and their fill (in-quota imports over the quota quantity) come from the 2022 WTO AG-IMS MA:2 notifications (`dataset/data/wto_agims_trq/`; JPN fiscal year 2022). The MAcMap bands set each quota line's regime: fill below 90 percent in-quota, above 98 percent over-quota, the mean in between; a quota with no notified imports is in-quota (`trq_no_fill`; BACI tonnes are not used because the quota units and HS6 tonnes do not match line by line). From the WITS bulk AVE row of the HS6 (sum, minimum, maximum and count of national lines), each of the k notified quota lines is taken as one in/over pair at the HS6 minimum and maximum (`trq_line_rates`): new = (sum - k(min + max) + sum of regime rates)/(n - k), clamped to [min, max]; a quota line at 4 or 5 digits (or in an older HS whose 8-digit line is not in the HS6 universe) covers the whole HS6 at its regime rate. A line is changed only where the held rate equals the bulk average and the line is not under another override; preference records from the bulk files are changed the same way, and where the preference in-quota lines are below the MFN ones on the same nomenclature and year (a lower HS6 minimum, or the same maximum and line count with a lower sum) the in-quota pair is bilateral (CUSMA, CETA, EFTA quotas) and every quota line takes the in-quota rate (`trq_bilateral_fill`; bilateral fills are not notified to the WTO). NOR (quota lines are product lines) and ISR (one rate per heading or no AVE) were checked and are not changed. `trq_fill_s10.csv` (private) has one row per importer, partner and HS6 with its status; `S10-Htrq` (hard) checks every applied rate on the schedule or preference record and inside the HS6 range; `S10-Rtrq` reports counts. A high-rate, year-jump or ceiling screen hit on a heading at its fill-rule rate is evidence class `trq` (as for Korea).
3. **Best applied at HS6.** For each importer, exporter and HS22 line: the minimum of MFN and every usable preference. Within one agreement family the latest year that has the line wins; across families the minimum wins (`agreement_families`). A preference with no MFN line is left out of both averages. Malaysia's 2022 preferences add its own schedules and gazetted orders to the 2014 TRAINS extract: the bilateral FTAs (MICECA, MJEPA, MPCEPA, MAFTA, MNZFTA, MCFTA, MTFTA), ACFTA, AHKFTA, ATIGA (P.U.(A) 199/2022), AKFTA, RCEP (from 18 March 2022), CPTPP (from 29 November 2022) and AJCEP (category end rates); each agreement's partner code, 2022 members and entry into force are in `mys_national_agreements.csv` (`mys_preference_schedules`; read by `tools/s10_extract_tariff_texts.py`). AANZFTA keeps its 2014 line (no schedule among the sources). Preference membership is the WITS beneficiary table, checked row by row against `preference_group_members.csv` (`S10-Hgroups`), with the 2022 rules: lapses, UK continuity, US GSP lapse, EU GSP graduation, Cambodia EBA withdrawal, CPTPP and RCEP membership. Customs unions (`customs_unions.csv`): intra-EU27 (and Monaco) is a forced zero; GCC, SACU, EAEU (BLR, KAZ, KGZ, RUS), EAC and Mercosur are a zero where no preference already wins, except Mercosur chapter 87 and sugar 1701–1703; the partial EU arrangements (SMR, AND from chapter 25, TUR industrial without coal and steel, EEA from chapter 25, CHE–LIE) apply on their goods.
4. **One code map.** Every source code (BACI numeric and ISO labels, TRAINS reporters, WITS numeric partners, group members) goes through `codes.CodeMap`, built from the S01 country map and its aliases (PSE→PAL, S19→TWN, XKX→KSV). `S10-Hjoin` fails on any code with trade, any reporter, or any member that does not reach a region.
5. **BACI weights** (`weights.py`, the 2021–2023 average as in ch.11B: BACI HS22 2022 and 2023, BACI HS17 2021 through the UNSD HS2022–HS2017 conversion table (a partition), split by 2022 world imports; `tariff_weights`, `S10-Hweightyears`, and `S10-Hweightconv`, which converts BACI HS17 2022 the same way and compares it with BACI HS22 2022 by sector). Each BACI flow is priced at its HS6 line rate and summed by member ISO into importer region × exporter region × sector. A flow inside one composite region is priced at its member-pair rate (`composite_diagonal_tariffs`); intra-EU is zero. Importer ISOs with no schedule are left out of the weight, except territories that apply a parent customs tariff (`importer_without_schedule`, `parent_customs_territory.csv`, David 2026-10-23): San Marino and Andorra are priced at the EUN schedule and the EU preferences on the goods their union with the EU covers (`S10-Hparent`). Turkmenistan has no rate table (Resolution 9925); TKM cells have tm 0 (`tkm_exception`).
6. **Cells with VCIF and no BACI weight** take the pair's own HS6 rates weighted by the importer's total HS6 imports (`missing_baci_weight`), else the simple mean over lines. A cell with neither is `missing` and has tm 0.
7. **Duty.** VCIF = S07R VFOB + S08 VTWR. VMSB = VCIF × (1 + tm). Duty = VCIF × tm. Section 301 and 232 are not in tm; `tariffs_301_232_addon_s10.csv` holds them as a scenario layer on the baseline VCIF (ch.11B §B.4). `S10-Hwedge` (fix list B9) reads the written tm, VMSB and duty files back and compares them with values the build did not write: every VCIF cell is written, the written VCIF equals S07 vfob + S08 vtwr re-read from the input files, and on a cell with a BACI weight the written rate equals the BACI line accumulator. Duty = VCIF × tm and VMSB ≥ VCIF are kept and labelled as the identity part.

## IO, support and taxes (ported unchanged after review)

Duty is removed from `io_s09` TLS at the importer × IO column effective rate (oil and gas into B06), capped at the column's import share of TLS (ch.17A p.2). The excess is reported, not netted (S13 FIT). Basic rows are byte-identical. The remaining TLS is split dom/imp by positive basic value.

Domestic support is OECD MONEVA 2022 budgetary A2–E on `crp`; MPS, PN and PM are excluded (ch.11A). EU MONEVA is split by FAOSTAT 2022 item 2051 (`pse_eu`; ch.13D's CAP Strategic Plans and State Aid Scoreboard member split is not in `dataset/data`). C and D are mapped by basis from their MONEVA splits, published in national currency (ch.11A p.2: area to land, animal numbers to capital, receipts and income to labour): PCAAN by PC_AREA/PC_AN (`pse_aan_split`, fix round w13; FAOSTAT crop/livestock output only where MONEVA has no complete split, none in 2022), PCRI by PC_R/PC_INC, PHR by PHR_AREA/AN/R/INC. E (PHNR) goes to all crp factors, one rate on crp value added (`pse_mapping`): the ch.13D §D.2 standard formula; GTAP's E proportions from the previous release are not available (departure logged). **EU members** (`pse_eu_mapping`, fix round w13, GTAP 12 ch.13D §D.3, QA finding #3): E (decoupled BISS, CRISS, CIS-YF, eco-schemes; 100 percent area-based in EU MONEVA 2022) and the income-based parts of C and D go to land only, one rate on crp land (one farm sector here), flag PHNR-EU-LAND and `fill` = `phnr_eu_land_ch13d` on its own `land` row. Rural-development area payments are C area payments and stay on land. A2–E is booked in full either way (`S10-Hpse`).

Export subsidies are positive WTO notifications at the notification-year WDI rate (ch.11C p.2), over the product's own HS exports; below 0.05 USD million of own exports, above a 100 percent rate, or above sector exports the row is reported and not booked.

**Export taxes** (fix list A2, PLAN.md:292; `export_taxes_other`, `s10_tariffs/export_tax.py`). The 2022 total is OECD Global Revenue Statistics T_5124 (taxes on exports, general government, USD; `dataset/data/oecd_grs/grs_rsglobal_5121_5124_2017_2022.csv`). It is spread over the exporter's taxed products in `config/s10/export_tax_products_2022.csv` (WTO Trade Policy Review lists: report, table and page on every row; `dataset/data/wto_tpr/`): each HS line takes the row with the longest matching prefix; its weight is the cited ad valorem rate (a specific or formula duty: the mean of the country's cited ad valorem rates) times its BACI 2022 exports; a sector with no S07 VFOB carries none. The sector rate is the sector's duty over its VFOB, the same to every destination. Booked: ARG, KAZ, IDN, MYS. A country with a total and no list, or a list and no 2022 total (TUR), is not booked and is named in `EXPORT-TAX-NEEDS-SOURCE` (`export_tax_unlisted`). **Rates path** (`export_tax_rates`, QA HIGH-2, 2026-10-24): RUS, IND and CHN have duties in force in 2022 and no GRS T_5124 2022 value, so `config/s10/export_tax_rates_2022.csv` gives one 2022 annual ad valorem rate (share of FOB) per exporter and HS prefix, each row with its legal basis, URL and retrieval date. Statutory ad valorem rates are taken as they are; specific and formula duties become equivalents on the exporter's BACI 2022 unit value (USD per tonne); part-year duties are time-weighted; groups that one HS6 line cannot separate (grades, national lines, product classes) take their simple mean (`dataset/data/export_duty_schedules_2022/derive_export_duty_aves_2022.py` writes `derived_aves_2022.csv`; a test checks the config carries the same rates). RUS: crude (monthly Minfin duty, mean 49.9 USD/t, 7.6%), products (Resolution 276 coefficients 30/30/55/100% of the crude duty, BACI 271000, 3.3%), pipeline gas 30% (Resolution 754), LNG 0, grain and oilseed duties, scrap and metal waste (OECD inventory). IND: iron ore and pellets (Notifications 28, 29 and 58/2022), steel 15% for 22 May-18 November, bauxite, ilmenite and rutile, ferrous scrap, rice 20% from 9 September (Notification 49/2022), and the special additional excise duty on diesel, ATF and petrol exports from 1 July. CHN: every 2022 measure of Customs Tariff Commission Announcement 18/2021 in the OECD Inventory of Export Restrictions (`dataset/data/oecd_export_restrictions/`); its ch.03, 05, 29 and 41 lines are not in the inventory and are not booked; the WTO TPR chapter rows of `export_tax_products_2022.csv` are kept for reference and skipped. The sector rate is the export-weighted mean of the line rates over all of the exporter's BACI 2022 lines of the sector; duty = rate x S07 VFOB, the same to every destination (Russian duties do not apply within the EAEU; not split). `S10-Rxtaxrev` (report) sets the implied revenue against IMF GFS G1152 2022 (`dataset/data/imf_gfs/`; RUS 2,730 bn RUB; CHN reports 0, IND none). `S10-Hxtax`: each GRS-booked exporter adds up to its GRS total and has a cited list; each rates-booked exporter is in the rates table with duty = rate x VFOB; no exporter is on both paths; every rate is in [0, 1). VXSB = VFOB / (1 − subsidy rate) − VFOB × export-tax rate (GTAP 12 ch.17A eq. 17.30); `vfob_s10.csv.gz` has an `export_tax_rate` column and `S10-Hvxsb` checks the wedge against the booked outlay less the booked tax. The coal, oil, gas and petroleum cells are re-priced by S11, which must carry the rate (`export_tax_s10.csv`).

**Energy taxes on the energy rows** (fix list A1, David's card 2026-10-24; follows GTAP 12 ch.12 §12.3.3, §12.3.9, §12.3.10 and §12.4.4, David 2026-10-24 "stick to GTAP"; `s10_tariffs/energy_tax.py`; `energy_tax_rows`, `energy_tax_year`, `energy_subsidies`, `energy_tax_fill`, `energy_tax_usa`). GTAP collects energy excise as specific rates by commodity and use class (§12.3.3; an unknown rate is zero), aggregates them over the energy sources with volume weights (§12.3.9), gives countries without data the rates of proxy countries (§12.3.10) and converts them to ad valorem with its own final prices (§12.4.4). S10 runs before S11 sets the energy prices, so S10 writes the specific rates and S13 converts them (s13 `energy_ad_valorem`). Source: OECD Net Effective Energy Rates (`dataset/data/oecd_neer/`, DF_NEERS 2021, the latest year at or before the 2022 base by the build's year rule (`energy_tax_year` = `neer_2021`; `S10-Hneeryear` checks that the years used are the `oecd_neer` year in `config/source_years.csv`); 2023 and the 2021–2023 mean are implemented and fail that check): fuel excise, carbon tax and electricity excise and, net of them, the fossil-fuel and electricity subsidies, in EUR/GJ times the tax base in TJ, at the Eurostat EUR/USD average of the year. ETS permit prices are left out. `energy_tax_s10.csv` has one row per region, NEERS sector (use class, mapped to IO columns by `config/s10/energy_tax_classes.csv`) and energy row (pet: gasoline, diesel, kerosene, fuel oil, LPG and road biofuels; coa; gas; ely): the net tax (`tax_musd` + `subsidy_musd`, the NEERS subsidy part kept for the B15 overlap), the base and its unit, and for a TJ base the specific rate per GJ (net and subsidy part). The electricity excise is recorded by NEERS on the power sector's fuel inputs; it is a tax on every ely buyer (class ALL), so its base is the S11 domestic use of electricity (`electricity_s11`, converted in S13). `dpabr` takes no energy rate. **USA** (DF_NEERS has no US values; `energy_tax_usa`, `energy_tax.us_motor_fuel`, fix round w7-integration): a source region, not a fill. Tax = (federal excise and LUST fee, 26 USC 4081 and 4041(d): 18.4 c/gal gasoline, 24.4 c/gal diesel, plus the EIA average state excise; mean of the rates in effect on 1 January and 1 July 2022, EIA Petroleum Marketing Monthly table, `dataset/data/eia_us_motor_fuel_taxes/`) × 2022 gallons (EIA MER T3.7c transportation motor gasoline; transportation distillate × the on-highway share of EIA FOKS 2020, `dataset/data/eia_foks/`), about 79.5 bn, on the ROAD class pet row, with the road-fuel barrels as the base (`million_bbl`; S13 converts barrels to GJ with the S11 products factor); the other US classes carry no energy tax (state utility and gross-receipts taxes are not sourced). `S10-Rusafuel` reports the parts. Any other region that is not a single country with source data gets one proxy row (class `*`): by default the S12 pool members on the same side of net energy trade (net exporter or importer of coa, oil, gas and pet by S10 VFOB), with their goods-trade weights (§12.3.10, David's card 2026-10-24 14:34 UTC); with none it carries no energy tax (§12.3.3). `S10-Henergy` (hard) checks that every source revenue is written once with its base and specific rate and that every fill row names source regions. Before fix round w12 the rate was the revenue over ICIO basic purchases with the column TLS cap (`energy_tax_cap`, `energy_gas_base`, now retired; the cap is the S13 `tax_rates` alternative). Eurostat 2022 household electricity non-VAT taxes are report-only (`S10-Renergyeu`). **Fuel sales taxes outside VAT and excise** (GTAP 12 ch.12 §12.3; `fuel_sales_taxes`, round-3 fix wE2): `config/s10/fuel_sales_taxes_2022.csv` holds sourced 2022 totals in national currency: India state sales tax/VAT on petroleum products (PPAC, contribution to state exchequer, calendar 2022 as a quarter of FY2021-22 plus three quarters of FY2022-23) and Brazil ICMS on fuels (Ministério da Fazenda total, Bahia not included). Each total is converted at the WDI 2022 rate and booked on the pet row of the ROAD class (households and road users) at the ROAD share of the country's NEERS TJ base for the named fuels (the rest is not booked and stays in the general rate); it adds to `tax_musd` and is written in `fuel_sales_tax_musd`, so S13 converts it with the excise and the column general rate (TLS less the energy taxes) falls by the same amount (column totals unchanged). Proxy regions take the excise only (S13 removes `fuel_sales_tax_musd` from the proxy rate). `S10-Rfuelsales` reports the totals and shares. PSE fertiliser and farm-power payments on the chm and ely rows need the OECD PSE country tables with PIV by input (`pse_input_rows`, NEEDS-SOURCE); PIV stays on the crp column.

**Commodity structure of the non-energy TLS** (QA round 2 P1, IMPROVEMENTS_LOG S13-2; choice `product_tax_pattern`, tagged `not_in_gtap_text`; `s10_tariffs/product_tax.py`). GTAP holds product-tax rates by commodity, source and agent (GTAP 12 ch.2 Table 2.3, VDFP/VDFB … VMPP/VMPB), taken from the national I-O valuation layers (ch.8). The ICIO gives one TLS per column, so S10 writes the layers S13 uses to place each column's non-energy TLS on its commodities; no column total, revenue or GDP moves here or in S13.
- `product_tax_pattern_s10.csv` (VAT layer): region × agent (`p`, `g`, `i`, `firm_exempt`, `firm`) × commodity, `vat_weight`. A single region with a schedule in `dataset/data/vat_statutory/` (OECD Consumption Tax Trends 2024 Annex Tables 2.A.1–2.A.3, the EC VAT-rate compilation, the Chinese VAT regulations; 38 countries) takes its latest schedule year at or before the base year; every other region the generic schedule: 1 outside the common exemptions of CTT Annex Table 2.A.3 note 2 (financial services, letting of immovable property, education, health, postal) and the non-market services (public administration, household employers). Households, government and investment pay the schedule (households 0 on rle, which is mostly the imputed rent of owner-occupied dwellings); an activity whose own output is exempt (`firm_exempt`) pays it on its inputs; other activities deduct VAT and get an indicator 1 on the non-exempt commodities (their remaining product taxes do not fall on the exempt services).
- `product_tax_layers_s10.csv` (revenue levels, USD million at percent of GDP times the pre-removal IO GDP, members GDP-weighted for a composite): VAT = OECD GRS T_5111, or for a region with no VAT (USA) T_5000 less T_5121, T_5123 and T_5124 (general and other consumption taxes); excise = T_5121; `energy_excise_musd` = the region's energy taxes in `energy_tax_s10.csv` (NEERS, EIA; blank for a fill region).
- `product_tax_control_s10.csv` (product control): the Eurostat supply-table taxes less subsidies on products (`naio_10_cp15`, D21X31, 2022; `dataset/data/eurostat_naio_product_taxes/`) by commodity group for the EU-27, NOR, ALB, SRB and MKD, as shares of the non-energy total (B05, B06, C19 and D are left out; a negative group, e.g. a product subsidy on land transport, is zero in the control and named in flag `PRODUCT-TAX-PATTERN`). `naio_10_cp1630` (by product and user) is confidential for Germany, so the product totals are used.
- `S10-Hptpattern` (hard): every region has a VAT weight in [0, 1] for each agent and commodity, the generic schedule puts 0 on the exempt and non-market services, the layers are finite and non-negative, and each region's control shares sum to 1. `S10-Rvat` now notes that T_5111 and T_5121 shape the structure only. Alternatives: `vat_schedule_grs_vat_and_excise_layers` (no Eurostat control) and `none` (one rate per column).

Income and factor taxes (GTAP 12 ch.15, general government including social security funds; `income_tax_source`, round-3 fix wE2): OECD GRS, then IMF WoRLD general government (S13), then IMF GFS Statement of Operations general government (S13, `dataset/data/imf_gfs/gfs_soo_income_ssc_gulf_2017_2023.csv`: G1111 PIT, G1112 CIT, G121 social contributions, G112+G113 payroll and property; latest year at or before 2022), then WoRLD central government (flagged; `GFS-GENERAL-GOV` lists the GFS uses). Saudi zakat sits in GFS G116 (other taxes) and is booked as CIT (`gulf_zakat`, our choice: zakat is levied on business net worth and income); GOSI contributions are the S1314 part of G121. QAT, KWT, BHR and OMN have no GFS general-government series and are unchanged; a total is split PIT/CIT at the covered shares; Bahrain and Kuwait book income tax as CIT; the simple average only where no source has the tax (`S10-Htaxsource`). Values are percent of GDP times pre-removal IO GDP.

## Checks and screens

Every hard check is a pure function in `s10_tariffs/checks.py` with a mutation test. The screens suite (SCREENS.md) runs on the same build objects and writes to `dataset/reviews/screens/S10/`, not to `output/`.

## Outputs

`dataset/stages/S10_tariffs_taxes/output/`. New in the fix round: `energy_tax_s10.csv` (private; it replaces `tls_energy_s10.csv`, fix round w12) and `export_tax_s10.csv`. New in round 3 (QA round 2 P1): `product_tax_pattern_s10.csv`, `product_tax_layers_s10.csv` and `product_tax_control_s10.csv` (public sources). TRAINS- and customs-derived files are listed in `NOT_FOR_PUBLICATION.md`. `price_basis_s10.csv` tags every file.

## Running

`cd dataset/build && python3 s10_tariffs_taxes.py` (about 20 minutes). One run peaks near 5 GB of memory, so run the R6 repeat runs one after another, not side by side, on a machine with less than 16 GB.
