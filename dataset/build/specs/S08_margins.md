# S08 international transport margins (VTWR, VST)

Spec for the 2022 build. Precedence: this file, then `PLAN.md` §3(d) as amended by E18 and E46, then `data-round/READINESS.md` §1 (S08 gate: CIF − FOB = margin; total margin supply = margin demand) and rule F7, `data-round/concepts.md` C03, then the GTAP texts cited below.

The stage reads the committed S07 and S01 snapshots. It does not rerun S01–S07. It does not rewrite VXSB. `io_s08.csv.gz` is a byte copy of `io_s07.csv.gz`.

`config/s08/margin_choices.csv` has one row per choice. The stage reads every value from that file. It does not choose and it does not change a row.

GTAP's margins chapter is not public. GTAP 5 ch.15.D (Gehlhar and McDougall) and GTAP 12 ch.10A have no public text on disk (checked 2026-10-21). Where a step below is not in the public chapters, the choice row says `not_in_gtap_text`.

## What GTAP says

GTAP 7 ch.9A p.3: "Merchandise trade flows in the GTAP Data Base explicitly take into account transport costs, represented by the difference between the c.i.f price and the f.o.b. price." The same page: transport margins "are developed from detailed bilateral commodity data from the United States. Import charges explicitly represent the aggregate cost of all freight, insurance and other related transport costs in the US data." Insurance is inside the margin.

GTAP 7 ch.9A p.6 §9.A.4: "The cif value is generated by reapplying the transport margins to the reconciled flows prior to aggregating to the GTAP sector level." The steps on that page end with "6. Reapply transport margin to create cif trade flows" and "7. Aggregate to GTAP sector level".

GTAP 12 ch.02 p.17 names `VCIF` (imports at CIF), `VST` (margin exports), `VTWR` (margins by margin commodity) and `VFOB` (non-margin exports at FOB). p.2: array `MARG` lists the margin commodities. p.16: `otp` land transport and pipelines (49), `wtp` water transport (50), `atp` air transport (51). Those are `rdt`, `sea` and `air`. p.5: import duties are VMSB/VCIF and export duties are VFOB/VXSB.

GTAP 12 ch.10B p.3 Table 10.2: SC11 and SC12 sea transport map to `wtp`; SC21 and SC22 air transport map to `atp`; SC31 and SC32 other transport map to `otp`.

GTAP 12 ch.17B §B.2 p.3: assembly merges FIT with the bilateral trade data. "international trade and transport margins data … are incorporated into the data base", and trade at basic prices is calculated from protection and from trade at CIF prices.

GTAP 12 ch.17A p.2 item 2: FIT holds the "Value of export and import vectors at border prices". A margin supply that exceeds a region's transport output is reconciled in the tables at S13. Trade is not changed here. That is the same logic as S07's hub cap (E45).

## What this stage does

The twelve rows in `margin_choices.csv` are the rules.

1. **Margin rate by product and route.** Source is OECD ITIC 2022, `PT_IM_CIF` (CIF−FOB as percent of CIF), HS 2017 4-digit, importer × exporter. The unit conversion is m = r / (1 − r), with r the CIF share (E18, C03). An HS6 BACI flow inherits its HS4 rate. The flows are then aggregated to S07 sectors with S01 `concord_hs17_to_sector.csv` shares, weighted by BACI HS17 2022 FOB values for that region pair (ch.9A p.6). BACI code 490 (ISO3 S19) is Taiwan, as in S01. ISL stays inside XWE.
2. **Fallback, in order, each flagged.** The pair's HS4 rate; ITIC's importer × world (`COUNTERPART_AREA=W`) HS4 rate; the pair's total (`_T`); the importer's world total (`_T`), labelled `importer_world_total` so it is not confused with the bilateral total; F7, the median HS4 rate over the importer's GTAP geographic region. F7 is last. A route with no BACI flow keeps the importer's BACI product weights and, on each weight, uses that route's own ITIC rates in the same order. CHE is an ITIC importer only from a few partners, so most of its imports take the importer-world rate. PRK and GNQ are not ITIC importers; they take F7. PAL's country-map code row has a blank geo; the S01 region registry records WEAS, and F7 uses that recorded region. ITIC is a modelled dataset (C03): about 94% of values are status I and about 6% are status A.
3. **Oil and gas** use the same HS4 file. HS 2709 is crude. HS 2711 is the rate on 271111 and 271121 (gas) and also on LPG (271112 and neighbours), which the concordance puts in `pet`. The flag is `LPG-IN-2711`. With `energy_sea_rate = us_census_exporter_hs4` (next round, Gulf margins check 2026-10-25): every 2022 ITIC value is imputed, and on routes not into the USA ITIC's crude and product rates run about 2.5-3x the US Census 2022 charges of the same exporters. The tanker part of `oil` (routes not in `energy_route_modes.csv`) and the sea part of `pet` take the exporter region's Census rate, m = (con_cif − con_val) / con_val over HS 2709 or 2710 (IMDB `hs6_cty_rp_2022.csv`, country names through the S01 map plus `CENSUS_ENERGY_ALIAS`); an exporter region with under 0.1 bn of US customs value takes the value-weighted rate of all exporters but Canada (oil 2.35%, pet 3.34%). On a route to an importer outside the Americas an exporter's own rate is floored at that fallback (`AMERICAS_GEO`; its US rate reflects its haul to the USA; S16 review, our choice): 3,265 routes of 34 exporters, +3.4 bn of VTWR (census_source `census_floor_outside_americas`). m = sea × Census + (rdt + air) × ITIC, and the route's mode shares are the parts of that m. Pipelines, the road and air parts, gas and every route into the USA keep ITIC. GTAP 7 ch.9A p.3 (margins from US import charges; the GTAP 12 margins chapter is not in the repo); the exporter's US rate on its routes to every importer is our choice, not in GTAP text. `itic` keeps ITIC unchanged (the beta v2 build). Output `energy_sea_rates_s08.csv`, hard check `S08-H13`, flag `ENERGY-SEA-CENSUS`, one ledger row per exporter and sector.
4. **Composite diagonal.** Member-pair rates on VXSB(r, r) for a composite region. A single-country diagonal is not in the S07 file, and H11 rejects VTWR on one. Not in GTAP text.
5. **VTWR by route.** The bound S07 snapshot is head `c8a2e29`, STAMP 2026-10-21 16:13:48 UTC+7, output SHA256SUMS `1a52be59d1c3e01ea21e8c2c1b7ab47aaf4c75f65ae79ed9abfd6bedf9e765b8`, `vxsb_goods_s07r.csv.gz` `7708ca32a5b3cb65871c9ebc9071138eb6882a3a6883fa39db64b13080ae695f`. `io_s07.csv.gz` is unchanged (`1ca75f62…`). The goods list is the S07 `goods_sectors` token, which now includes `wat`, `pub`, `art`, `pro` and `pst`. Those five use the same HS4 ladder. `margin_base` accepts only `s07r_vfob_baci`. The base column is `vfob_musd` (equal to `vxsb_musd`, `pre_tax=yes`). The retired `s07_vxsb_value` path is rejected. Sum over source first. VTWR[c,s,d] = m[c,s,d] × that sum. The US import and export totals skip exporter = importer. S10 still forms VCIF = VFOB + Σ VTWR. Where S10 adds export taxes, the implied rate on VFOB sits slightly below ITIC. Logged, not in GTAP text (E46).
6. **Mode split.** VTWR[m,c,s,d] = VTWR[c,s,d] × share[m,c]. With `mode_split=us_census_charges_by_sector`, the shares are US Census 2022 import charges by HS6, mapped with S01 `concord_hs22_to_sector.csv`: vessel charges to `sea`, air charges to `air`, the rest of general-import charges to `rdt`. One US mix is applied on every route. Not in GTAP text. The mix is driven by Canadian pipeline imports, so `rdt` is overstated for oil and gas and likely understated for manufactures. The stage reports it and does not rescale. The other allowed value, `tismos_world_freight`, would use one world split from TiSMoS 2022 mode-1 freight exports.
7. **VST.** VST[m,r] = Σ VTWR[m] × r's share of world freight exports of m. TiSMoS 2022 mode 1, partner World: SC12 sea, SC22 air, SC32 other (ch.10B p.3). Single economies map to S01 regions; composites are summed. A reporter with no TiSMoS freight row takes F8: the geographic region's TiSMoS freight / BaTIS SC ratio, applied to that reporter's BaTIS SC1, SC2 or SC3 exports to the world. Σ VST[m] = Σ VTWR[m] by construction. The allocation key is not in GTAP text. Since 2026-10-24 (`vst_allocation=..._f8_where_zero`, David in the S15 thread) a zero TiSMoS freight cell counts as missing and takes F8 per mode; the geographic ratio uses only reporters with positive freight in the mode, and a geographic region with none takes the world ratio. Every region then supplies every margin, as in GTAP 10 (the Julia model takes log of each region's margin supply). Dropping the zero reporters raises some geographic ratios (WEAS sea 0.722 → 0.983, WAFR sea 0.133 → 0.476), and BaTIS books flag registries as transport (LBR SC1 4.8 bn), so the uncapped fill gave LBR 5.2 bn on a 4.0 bn GDP (S08 VST-fix QA B1). Each filled cell is therefore capped at the region's own I-O exports of the mode (io_s07 row dom_<m>, column X), with a floor of 0.01 USD m; the excess goes to the other cells in proportion, so Σ VST[m] = Σ VTWR[m] still holds (`vst_allocation=..._f8_where_zero_capped`, David's S08 card in the S15 thread, 2026-10-24; flag `VST-F8-CAP`, ledger rows per capped cell).
8. **No cap and no I-O change.** VST above transport output is report-only (`VST-GT-OUTPUT`). S13 FIT reconciles it (ch.17A p.2). `io_s08.csv.gz` is a byte copy of `io_s07.csv.gz`. The freight double count with ICIO transport-services trade is removed at S09, not here (PLAN §3(e), E46). `freight_for_s09.csv` carries VST[m,r] and the sum of VTWR[m] into each importer. Services margins are zero (PLAN §3(e)).

Not done here: VFOB, VCIF, tariffs, any rescaling of ITIC other than the `energy_sea_rate` override of step 3, any cap of VST on output.

## Outputs

Under `dataset/stages/S08_margins/output/`:

- `margin_rates_s08.csv.gz`: sector, exporter, importer, r_cif, m_fob, level (`pair_hs4`, `importer_world_hs4`, `pair_total`, `importer_world_total`, `F7`), and the share of BACI weight at each level.
- `vtwr_s08.csv.gz`: margin (`rdt`, `sea`, `air`), sector, exporter, importer, vtwr_musd.
- `vst_s08.csv`: margin, region, vst_musd, key_musd, key_source per cell (`tismos`, `F8`, or `tismos+F8` for a composite with both).
- `freight_for_s09.csv`: `record` `vst` or `vtwr_into`, margin, region, musd.
- `usa_margins_s08.csv`: US import margin by sector (ITIC against Census charges/customs value), top partners, USA VST, USA export rates, USA oil and gas.
- `io_s08.csv.gz`: byte copy of `io_s07.csv.gz`.

The stage kit writes STAMP, SHA256SUMS, checks, flags, the ledger (every fallback level with its value), inputs.lock, NEEDS_DAVID.md, REVIEW_BRIEF.md and build.log. Times are UTC+7. Gzip uses mtime 0.

## Checks

Hard, all required for the STAMP: S08-G0 (kit; bound to S07 head `c8a2e29`, STAMP 2026-10-21 16:13:48 UTC+7, output SHA256SUMS `1a52be59d1c3e01ea21e8c2c1b7ab47aaf4c75f65ae79ed9abfd6bedf9e765b8`, goods file `7708ca32a5b3cb65871c9ebc9071138eb6882a3a6883fa39db64b13080ae695f`), S08-G0c (kit, report), S08-IN1, S08-IN2, then H1 through H11. H1 is the eleven choice rows. `margin_base` must be `s07r_vfob_baci`. H2 is a rate on every positive goods key. H3 is 0 ≤ r < 1 and a finite m = r/(1−r). H4 is VTWR = m × VXSB and mode shares. H5 is supply equals use by margin commodity. H6 is a non-negative VST on S01 regions. H7 is the byte copy and no VXSB rewrite. H8 is the S09 file. H9 is the licence list: no BACI zip, ITIC bulk, TiSMoS zip, HS17 concordance or GTAP text in git. H10 is that no route or cell uses F7 while an ITIC pair or importer-world rate exists. Pair-basket cells that have already missed pair HS4, importer-world HS4 and the bilateral total stay on F7. H11 is no VTWR on (r, r) for a single-country region.

Report: R1 world VTWR against the preview. If the preview CSV is absent, the check uses the committed constants 1,050.8 bn and 6.23% of FOB. The preview is not a target. R2 Korean crude from Saudi Arabia. R3 fallback levels. R4 the USA file. R5 VST against TiSMoS freight exports and against ICIO transport output. R6 two runs, `GTAP_STAGES_DIR`. R7 the trade-weighted distribution of the margin.

## Source differences found in the fix round (B11, 2026-10-24; no rule changed)

The S16 GTAP 10 comparison flags low margin rates on USA gas imports and on bulk goods (crp, nmm, stl, pet) in several large economies. S08's rules match what this spec says the stage does: pipelines are a margin (`rdt`, GTAP's `otp`, through the Census mode split), and every rate is ITIC's own, read through the fallback ladder. GTAP 12 ch.10A has no public text on the margin method, and `margin_source` (ITIC in place of GTAP's US-data estimates) is already a logged departure. The low rates come from two properties of the ITIC source. Neither is changed here, because each fix needs a new choice row from David.

1. **ITIC publishes exactly zero on neighbouring EU pairs.** 160 of 41,238 ITIC 2022 pair totals are 0 (70 of them intra-EU: FRA←BEL, FRA←DEU, FRA←ITA, DEU←NLD, DEU←BEL, DEU←FRA, …), and the HS4 rows of those pairs are 0 or within 1e-4 of it. H3 accepts r = 0, so 4,245 route cells carry 1,654 bn of FOB (7.2% of world goods trade) at a zero margin. That is 44% of FRA's imports, 35% of DEU's, 58% of AUT's and 22% of NLD's. Trade-weighted import margins are then FRA stl 1.3%, DEU stl 2.5%, DEU pet 2.4% and FRA nmm 3.6%, against 6–12% for the same goods into CHN, JPN, KOR and IND. Also among the zero-rate pairs: BEL→DEU gas (28.6 bn) and BEL→FRA gas (17.0 bn).
   - *Proposed fix (our choice; no GTAP text).* Treat a published zero pair rate as missing, so the cell takes the next rung of the ladder (importer × world HS4). This is how `vst_allocation` already treats a zero TiSMoS freight cell (David, S15 thread, 2026-10-24). A rough proxy uses the importer's FOB-weighted rate over its partners with a positive rate. On that proxy, FRA's import margin goes from 3.6% to 6.4%, DEU's from 3.8% to 5.9% and AUT's from 2.3% to 5.6%, and world VTWR rises by about 114 bn. The proxy is an upper bound, because importer-world rates include distant partners. A neighbour-specific fill, such as the importer's rate from its other contiguous partners, would be closer, but no source for it is on disk. Suggested choice row: `itic_zero_rate`, values `accept` (now) or `missing`.
2. **Pipeline gas into the USA.** ITIC gives CAN→USA HS 2711 r = 1.24% of CIF, so the USA gas import margin is 1.3% (0.27 bn on 20.8 bn). The US Census 2022 import charges in `census_imports_hs_2022`, which are the source GTAP 7 ch.9A p.3 names for US margins, are 2.46 bn on a customs value of 16.46 bn for HS 271121 (gaseous natural gas, almost all pipeline from Canada; vessel value 7,680 USD). That is 14.9%. The gap is already printed in `usa_margins_s08.csv` (ITIC 1.3% against Census 14.0% for the gas sector, a gap above the 1.5-point line). Other large pipeline routes take 6–12% from ITIC (NOR→DEU 6.5%, DZA→ITA 9.0%, RUS→ITA 9.0%), so the low rate is specific to CAN→USA.
   - *Proposed fix (follows GTAP's US source).* For importer USA and HS 271121, take m = Census GEN_CHA_YR / GEN_VAL_YR (charges per customs value are already a rate on FOB) in place of the ITIC rate, and book the margin to `rdt` (pipelines) as now. On the S07 base, USA gas VTWR rises from 0.27 bn to about 2.8 bn. Suggested choice row: `usa_pipeline_gas_rate`, values `itic` (now) or `census_271121_charges`.
