Physical output for crude, natural gas and refined products, 2022.

The builder reads production_2022.csv through earlier-build/build/energy_physical.jl.
Volumes are US EIA International Energy Statistics, bulk file INTL.zip
(https://www.eia.gov/opendata/bulk/INTL.zip), downloaded 2026-10-20, zip last-modified
2026-10-19. The bulk file is not committed.

Oil volume: INTL.57-1 crude including lease condensate plus INTL.58-1 natural gas
plant liquids, annual 2022, thousand barrels per day times 1000, stored as barrels
per day. The price is not one Brent marker. It is the nearest of the four spot
crudes in the Energy Institute Statistical Review of World Energy, 2025 edition,
sheet "Spot crude prices", year 2022 (workbook downloaded 2026-10-20 from
https://www.energyinst.org/__data/assets/excel_doc/0008/1656215/EI-Stats-Review-ALL-data.xlsx;
the workbook is not committed):

- Dubai dated 96.38 USD/bbl for the Middle East and Asia-Pacific
- Brent dated 101.32 USD/bbl for Europe, Africa except Nigeria, and the former Soviet Union
- Nigerian Forcados 101.40 USD/bbl for Nigeria
- WTI Cushing spot 94.58 USD/bbl for the United States, Canada and Mexico

The sheet's source line is S&P Global Commodity Insights, copyright 2025. Footnotes
on that sheet: 1972-1985 Arabian Light, 1986-2024 Dubai dated; 1976-1983 Forties,
1984-2024 Brent dated; Forcados FOB Nigeria; 1976-1983 posted WTI, 1984-2024 spot
WTI (Cushing). The EIA volume includes NGPL. The EI sheet does not price NGPL, so
the crude marker is applied to the combined barrel and the note on each oil row
says so.

Gas volume: INTL.26-1 dry natural gas, billion cubic metres. The United States is
priced at the EIA Henry Hub annual 2022 price, 6.45 USD/MMBtu, series RNGWHHD,
https://www.eia.gov/dnav/ng/hist_xls/RNGWHHDa.xls, annual row dated 2022-06-30.
Every other gas row, including every Gulf producer, has no price. A Gulf price
has to be an official regulated price (government decree, ministry or regulator
notice, or national oil company annual report or prospectus) for 2022, or the
latest earlier year still in force in 2022, with document, page and date. Those
documents were not obtained with a page-level price. Export prices and Henry Hub
were not copied onto the Gulf. The build stops on a missing price
(ENERGY_PRICE_MISSING).

The IEA Fossil Fuel Subsidies Database, 2025 edition (file "Subsidies 2010-2024.xlsx",
updated 13/11/2025, CC BY 4.0) has no domestic price column. It is used only as
the cross-check in gulf_gas_subsidy_crosscheck_2022.csv. See
PROVENANCE_SUBSIDY_CROSSCHECK.txt. It does not fill a price.

Refined volume: INTL.54-1 refined petroleum products, thousand barrels per day
times 1000. The 2025 EI workbook does not publish Rotterdam, Singapore or US Gulf
product spot prices (gasoline, gas oil, fuel oil). It publishes crude spots and
regional refining margins. OPEC ASB was not available. Refined rows have no price.
They are not valued at Brent. The build stops until a product price is added.

Valuation method, when a price is present: McDougall and Chepeliev, Journal of
Global Economic Analysis, 2021. The booking of a regulated price and a subsidy is
energy_booking.jl. Aguiar, Chepeliev, Corong and van der Mensbrugghe, Journal of
Global Economic Analysis 7(2), 2022, section 3.2.3, is the treatment of flaring
(marketed gas excludes gas flared), not the price.

Cross-check of 2022 crude and condensate, thousand barrels per day, EI sheet
"Crude+cond production - barrels" against EIA INTL.57-1 (this file's crude
component, not the NGPL add-on):

- Saudi Arabia EI 10508.8, EIA crude 10616.7
- United Arab Emirates EI 3463.8, EIA crude 3683.4
- Qatar EI 1267.5, EIA crude 1269.8
- Kuwait EI 2707.0, EIA crude 2795.1
- Iraq EI 4445.6, EIA crude 4503.5
- Oman EI 1064.2, EIA crude 1063.3
- Iran EI 3312.0, EIA crude 3116.5

The volumes in the CSV stay on the EIA series. The EI figures are the check,
not a replacement. Iran differs by about 6 percent; that is reported and not
averaged away.

The gas unit conversion, used only when a row prices gas in USD per MMBtu and
states volume in billion cubic metres, is the EIA thermal factor: 1 cubic metre
is 35.3147 cubic feet and 1 thousand cubic feet of dry gas is 1.037 million Btu
(EIA Energy Conversion Calculators).
